Weather Data Interpolation Techniques

Inverse Distance Weighting (IDW) is a spatial interpolation method that estimates
values at unmeasured locations using a weighted average of nearby known points.
Points closer to the target location are given more weight than points farther
away, based on the assumption that closer things are more similar.

Kriging is a more advanced geostatistical interpolation method that accounts for
both the distance and the degree of spatial correlation between known data
points. Unlike IDW, Kriging provides an estimate of prediction uncertainty at
each interpolated point, making it useful when data confidence matters.

Data Imputation Methods

KNN (K-Nearest Neighbors) imputation fills in missing values by looking at the
K most similar data points (neighbors) and using their average or most common
value. It works well when data has clear local patterns.

Iterative Imputation models each feature with missing values as a function of
other features, repeatedly refining estimates until they converge. This tends
to work better than simple mean imputation when features are correlated with
each other.

Transformer Fine-Tuning

Fine-tuning takes a pre-trained transformer model and further trains it on a
smaller, task-specific dataset. This adapts the model's general language
understanding to a specific domain (like climate data) without training a
model from scratch, which would require far more data and compute.
